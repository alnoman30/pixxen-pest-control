The real problems

Nine reasons most pest control websites lose homeowners to Orkin and Terminix

Independent pest control companies compete against Orkin, Terminix, Rollins, and dozens of franchises spending millions on national SEO. Most independent pest control sites we audit are leaking qualified service calls.

01

No project gallery — or worse, a generic one

Homeowners need to see your actual work, organized by type (patio, deck, full backyard, front yard, garden, hardscape), before they trust you with a $25K project.

02

Hidden pricing on recurring plans

Quarterly, bi-monthly, monthly — these are the LTV plays in pest control. Hiding pricing kills the comparison-shopper who wants to budget recurring household expenses.

03

Generic 'Services' page where pest-specific pages should live

Roaches, ants, spiders, termites, bed bugs, rodents, mosquitoes — each is a separate search intent with different urgency. One page ranks for none.

04

No discreet contact for bed bug customers

Bed bug customers are deeply private — they don't want neighbors knowing. Sites without discreet booking flow lose this segment to companies that make discretion explicit.

05

EPA and state license info buried

State pest control license, EPA registration, applicator certifications — trust signals homeowners shopping for chemical treatments actively look for.

06

No eco-friendly or green-product positioning

30% of pest control prospects (families with young children, pet owners, chemically sensitive adults) filter on green products. Sites without IPM or 'pet-safe' messaging lose this segment.

07

Treatment process not explained

'How long does treatment take?' 'When can pets come back inside?' 'Will it smell?' — homeowners research process before booking. Sites that answer these win the research-stage customer.

08

No service area map

'Serving greater [city]' wastes dispatch on out-of-zone calls. Specific ZIPs and a map filter properly and prevent the truck-roll-to-cancel waste.

09

Reviews on Google but not on the site

You have 4.7 stars on Google and 280 reviews — the homepage shows zero. Prospects won't open a new tab to verify.

Nine features pest control owners actually use and most websites skip

Independent pest control companies compete against Orkin, Terminix, Rollins, and dozens of franchises spending millions on national SEO. Most independent pest control sites we audit are leaking qualified service calls.

Same-day service availability indicator

Visible 'same-day appointments available' or 'next-day service' messaging in the hero, wins the urgent-pest homeowner who'd otherwise call the chain.

Recurring plan transparency

Quarterly, bi-monthly, monthly recurring plans with what's covered, frequency, and member-only pricing. Filters one-time shoppers and qualifies recurring customers.

Pest-by-pest landing pages

Roaches, ants, spiders, termites, bed bugs, rodents, mosquitoes, wildlife — each gets its own page with treatment approach, timeline, and pricing structure.

Discreet bed bug contact

Dedicated bed bug landing page with discreet contact form, unmarked-vehicle service messaging, K9 inspection availability, heat treatment options.

State license and EPA registration prominent

State pest control license number, EPA registration, applicator certifications — visible in the header where prospects look first.

Eco-friendly and IPM messaging

Integrated Pest Management (IPM) certification, EPA-registered products, pet-safe and child-safe formulations, organic-option pricing tiers.

Treatment process explanation

Pre-treatment prep, what happens during the visit, post-treatment guidance, re-entry timing, follow-up schedule. Answers silent objections.

Service area map with response time

Specific ZIP coverage with response-time commitment. Filters out-of-zone calls and signals reliability.

Live review aggregation

Real Google and Yelp reviews pulled live, embedded on homepage and pest-specific pages. Schema-marked for rich snippets.
